## Modern English translation

[Continuation from PDF page 78]

I set out for the gold mines, thinking that I could earn money to pay our debt, but I did not go there. I remained at Fort Bridger to work for the soldiers and build some [unclear in Welsh]. I then returned to my mother, my brothers, and my sisters, and nearly all our debt was paid. My brother had earned some wheat.

At the end of October I set out to look for land that could be cultivated so that we could raise our own bread and potatoes. I stayed in Cache Valley, [unclear in Welsh] at Logan, and worked there for a short time. My brother Thomas came after me and made an agreement with a man named Shepherd. [The following travel and employment wording is unclear in Welsh.]

Thomas and I returned home after February in the year 1860. We had to remain there because our animals were weak and we had little fodder for them. At the end of March we began our journey again. Daniel remained in Farmington to work and came after us shortly afterward. We raised only a small crop: 59 bushels of wheat, 4 bushels of barley, 15 bushels [unclear crop], 10 bushels of potatoes, and a few other things. We have prospered [continues on PDF page 80].

## Editorial annotations

First direct visual transcription and modern translation completed. Several words in the employment, return journey, and crop list remain uncertain.

The passage corresponds to RDR PDF page 9 with confirmed confidence and is a close historical translation with paraphrase and abridgment. RDR gives a clearer sequence for Shepherd, the sheep-driving work, and the return to Cache Valley, but those details have not been forced into uncertain Welsh letterforms. RDR also differs in the crop quantities and labels; the Welsh page is preserved independently pending review.

Robert D. Roberts is the probable content author and scribe based on the same first-person and handwriting continuity as pages 77-78, reinforced by the matching RDR narrative and nearby signatures.
