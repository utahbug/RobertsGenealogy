### Welsh entry 3

We arrived on the second day of October. This journey was very hard because we did not have half enough food, and it was hard work pulling the handcart. We stayed in this place for a few days. My father and the family went to a place called Farmington, eighteen miles to the north. [Unclear: Then I agreed] to go to Ogden with Erastus Bingham, twenty-two miles farther, and we were there for four months. I returned to my father. The following summer I went to Fort Elder, to Capt. Evans, who had married my uncle Daniel's wife, and [unclear: I worked there] that summer [unclear: afterward, Millcreek in Utah ...].

## Editorial annotations

Page 76 contains three distinct blocks and does not continue page 75.

The first two blocks are separate English account/debt acknowledgments dated April 6, 1858, one addressed to William Richards and one to Will Smith. Each contains first-person self-identification and ends with the exact signature `David R Roberts`; that signature appears twice as a governing signature, in addition to name occurrences in the headings and promises. The hand matches the signed February 4, 1858 entry on page 73 and the English genealogy records on page 74 in its capital D and R, slant, spacing, terminal strokes, numeral forms, and signature construction. David R Roberts is the confirmed content author and scribe of both contemporaneous English blocks. No clear RDR parallel was found for either account.

The source changes from English to Welsh immediately after the second `David R Roberts` signature. The lower Welsh block is in a different, lighter and more compressed hand matching the Robert D Roberts narrative on page 77 rather than the heavy page 74-75 farm-register hand. It is a first-person retrospective account of the October 2, 1856 arrival and early residence/work in Farmington, Ogden, and Fort Elder. Robert D Roberts is the probable content author and scribe, based on handwriting continuity, first-person family perspective, and the matching signed/datable sequence beginning on page 77.

The Welsh block ends unfinished near a reference to Millcreek. Page 77 does not continue its final sentence: it begins independently with `Logan City / January 2, 1861` and restarts the emigration history from departure in Wales. Page 76 is therefore best understood as a rough or partial retrospective version, while page 77 begins a fresh, fuller account. No page-77 correction is required.

RDR page 7 is a confirmed close-translation/later-copy parallel for the Welsh migration block. It preserves the same sequence: October 2 arrival in Salt Lake City, the hard handcart journey and inadequate food, the family's move to Farmington, Robert's agreement with Erastus Bingham and four months in Ogden, and the following summer with Capt. Evans at Fort Elder. RDR is smoother and more explicit; it was not used to fill the damaged ending or force uncertain Welsh letterforms.

The two signed English entries provide directly attributable David R Roberts handwriting samples. Their agreement with the signed page-73 sample and the self-identified page-74 English entries confirms the David Roberts scribal attribution of pages 68-72 within the limits of historical manuscript-image comparison.
