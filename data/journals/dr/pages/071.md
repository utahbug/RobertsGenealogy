## Modern English translation

[Unclear: the grandmother] of Gwilym Ystradau the Bard, where he too was lodging. He was nearly the same age and had the same inclination toward poetry; therefore we were of some encouragement to one another. We became acquainted with the old bard [unclear: Gryffyn Davy] and received some lessons from him.

Bethania, January 18, 1856

I was twenty-three years old when I married Catherine. We went to live at [unclear: Haffty y Moelwyn] Park, Llanfrothen, for the first year. From there we moved to Gelli Cwmstradlyn, in the same parish and on the same estate; from Gelli to [unclear place], parish of Festiniog; from [that place] to Dol y Garreg Ddu in the same parish; and from Dol y Garreg Ddu to Bethania, where we are now. We intend to emigrate to America (or Utah) between now and the beginning of next March.

I was brought to faith in the Gospel by Robert Evans of Glan Conwy and was baptized by him in the River Pandy near Gelli Cwmstradlyn on May 24, 1846. I was confirmed by Abel Evans, and soon received the Holy Priesthood. The important duty of preaching the everlasting Gospel in the fulness of its blessings was placed upon my shoulders. A branch of the Church of Jesus Christ called the Festiniog Branch was entrusted to my care. I had the privilege of lodging many servants of Christ in my house and assisting them with clothing and money to build the Kingdom of Jesus Christ my Lord.

My wife Chathreina was baptized by David Peters near Rhydfudr, Festiniog, on August 22, 1846.

Our children were baptized by me: Thomas, September 30, 1849; Daniel was baptized September 17, 1850, at eight years old; and Ann was baptized December 28, 1852, at eight years old. [Continues on PDF page 72.]

## Editorial annotations

The opening paragraph completes the sentence begun on page 70. David was lodging with the probable grandmother of `Gwilym Ystradau y Bardd`; the two young men shared an inclination toward poetry, encouraged one another, and received lessons from an older bard whose name remains uncertain. This is evidence of bardic instruction, but it does not identify a hymn, publication, or connection to the 1852 hymn attributed to `D. R.`.

The heading `Bethania Ionawr 18 - 1856` begins a distinct dated autobiographical entry. In this manuscript it functions as a residence/place-and-date heading: David says that he and his household are then living at Bethania and plan to emigrate to America or Utah. The manuscript does not independently establish whether `Bethania` denotes a house, chapel, meetinghouse, or broader locality, so no narrower identification is imposed.

The dated entry records household moves, conversion and baptism, confirmation, receipt of priesthood, preaching responsibility, care of the Festiniog Branch, support for traveling church servants, and family baptisms. It supplies direct first-person evidence for David's leadership of the Festiniog Branch. The baptism date of May 24, 1846 is present in the Welsh manuscript itself and is therefore transcribed independently of the external Ffestiniog membership-register lead.

RDR helped establish syntax and event order, but the uncertain bard name and two residence forms were not forced from its English.

Coherent comparison across pages 68-72 confirms the repeated `oeddwn` in the marriage-age sentence and improves the first residence candidate from `Haffty y Ty Mawr` to `[unclear: Haffty y Moelwyn] Park`. The older bard's name and the second residence remain unresolved.

The same compact Welsh hand and ink continue from pages 68-70. First-person self-reference, the contemporaneous 1856 heading, and autobiographical continuity support David Roberts as probable content author and probable scribe. There is no signature or independently attributable handwriting sample to justify confirmed attribution.
