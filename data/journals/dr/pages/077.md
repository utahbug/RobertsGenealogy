## Modern English translation

Logan City, January 2, 1861.

I will give a little of our history from the old country up to the present, as correctly as my memory allows. We left Bethania, Ffestiniog, on April 14, 1856, and reached Liverpool. On the nineteenth of the same month we went aboard the ship *S. Curling*, which began its journey across the Atlantic the following day. The voyage was very rough, with the sea rising greatly. We reached the American continent on May 24, [unclear in Welsh] Boston. After a day or two we took the railroad to Iowa City, where we remained for nearly three weeks. From there we began our journey across the plains with handcarts to Utah.

We stayed for a few days in Salt Lake City after arriving on October 2. The journey was very hard because we did not have half enough food, and pulling the handcart was hard. After several days my father and the [unclear in Welsh] went to Farmington, eighteen miles north of Salt Lake City. I then went twenty-two miles farther to Ogden with Erastus Bingham and remained there for four [unclear in Welsh], after which I returned to my father, my mother, my brothers, and my sisters.

## Editorial annotations

First direct visual transcription and modern translation completed. Several readings remain uncertain and are marked rather than supplied from the English witness.

The passage corresponds to RDR PDF page 7 with confirmed confidence. RDR is a close historical translation in overall event order, but it also paraphrases and abridges the Welsh. It supplies smoother relationship and travel wording, expands the handcart hardship, and continues the final sentence onto its next page. It was used as a concordance only, not as the base text.

The page is dated January 2, 1861, after David Roberts's death on November 4, 1858. Robert D. Roberts is the probable content author and scribe based on the first-person transition, the matching RDR passage, handwriting continuity, and explicit R. D. Roberts signatures in the continuing sequence. The date alone is not treated as proof of scribal identity.
