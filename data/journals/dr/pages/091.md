Medi 4, 1877

Taliesin Hughes a'i chwaer Maria Roberts, a'r eneth ieuanc Elizabeth ein chwaer, a merch ifanc o'r enw Jane Mason, tua deunaw mlwydd ei hoed, a briodwyd hwy gan William B. Preston yn ei dy. Dychwelodd a chyrhaeddasant hyd mor bell a Hamilton Station i dy Ann ein chwaer, lle yr oedd Jane ein chwaer.

Looking at her husband and her little children, the house and surroundings where she had moved about and sought to make herself, her husband, and her children comfortable, her breast was cold, her tongue silent and her lips closed, her appearance and expression gone, the spirit having risen and fled away, leaving only poor, cold earth to look upon, and it was time to lay it to rest before it crumbled. September 4, 1877

Taliesin Hughes and his sister Maria Roberts, with the young daughter of our sister Elizabeth, and a young woman named Jane Mason, about eighteen years old, were married by William B. Preston in his house. They returned as far as Hamilton Station, to the house of Ann, our sister, where Jane, our sister, was staying.

## Editorial annotations

- The opening completes Elizabeth Hughes's death, funeral, and return-journey narrative from page 90. It has a confirmed close-paraphrase parallel on RDR PDF page 18.
- The manuscript clearly preserves `Square Town`; RDR uses lowercase `square town`. The existing RDR annotation identifies this historically with the early settlement later known as Plymouth, but that identification is not inserted into the Welsh transcription.
- The sentence concerning the return journey, Conar Hughes's boy, and the broken arm is syntactically dense and remains partly uncertain. RDR clarifies the event but cannot determine every Welsh letterform or clause boundary.
- Targeted enlarged reinspection resolves the family-summary phrase as `tri o fechgyn a phedair o ferched` (three boys and four girls), agreeing with the RDR historical parallel. The earlier draft's “three pairs of twins” was a transcription error and has been corrected from the Welsh letterforms.
- The September 1877 marriage entry is separate and has a confirmed close-paraphrase parallel on RDR PDF page 19. RDR supplies smoother relationship wording but does not replace the Welsh source.
- Handwriting and first-person family continuity support probable attribution to Robert D. Roberts as content author and scribe. No signature or new hand appears.
