## Modern English translation

and Daniel. Daniel died in America. [Unclear: Ebenezer and the youngest was born in America and did not live there]; [unclear: they also belong to the Calvinistic Methodists].

Now I shall think a little about my own history.

I was raised amid great poverty. The ordinary laborer's wage was my father's wage, which for many years was from one shilling to one shilling and sixpence a day, while also feeding himself; this was often too little to obtain bread for the family. My tasks until I was about eleven were [unclear: begging or driving], gathering lichen, scraping oak bark, and [unclear occupational term], and similar things, each in its season.

I was very ill with ague when I was between ten and eleven, and soon after recovering I went to the quarry with my father and my brother Robert at Festiniog. I continued the work of dressing slate under many disadvantages until I was fifteen or sixteen. At that time I went to a place called Cae, next to the Cwmstradlyn valley, to work for a man named William Hughes. I remained there about a year and a half, then returned to the quarry; and from then until now I have followed one branch or another of quarry work.

No one considered [unclear: that I needed day school], and I received very little of it. My parents sent me to the Calvinistic Methodist Sunday school, where I began to read Welsh. My inclination was toward the Baptists, and consequently I was [unclear: resistant] to attending their schools; and the Baptists did not [unclear: maintain] a school, because they considered [unclear: teaching] a sin.

## Editorial annotations

The autobiography then treats childhood poverty, seasonal work, illness, slate quarrying, limited schooling, Calvinistic Methodist Sunday school, Baptist sympathies, and an early desire to become a poet.

RDR PDF page 4 is a confirmed close translation/paraphrase of the family notice and autobiographical transition. The page contains direct first-person self-reference and continues the same compact Welsh hand and ink used on pages 68-69. Feature-by-feature comparison with the directly signed `David R Roberts` entries dated February 4 and April 6, 1858 confirms David Roberts as content author and scribe within the limits of historical manuscript-image comparison.
