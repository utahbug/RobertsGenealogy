# David Roberts / Robert D. Roberts Welsh Journal: Consolidated Specialist Review

## 1. Purpose and scope

This document accompanies `DR Journal.pdf`, whose substantive family-history and journal block occupies PDF pages 68–109. All 42 pages in that range have complete first-pass handling. Welsh manuscript transcription, modern English translation, original-English manuscript entries, the later Robert D. Roberts historical-English journal (RDR), and editorial annotation are kept as distinct evidence layers.

The editorial goal is faithful transcription rather than silent modernization. Historical spelling, names, dates, places, grammar, list order, and uncertainty markers are retained. This package is intended for final scholarly and Welsh-language review after assembly of the product. It is not an invitation to reconstruct erased or damaged text beyond what survives in the manuscript image.

`Needs-review` does not mean untranscribed. It means that a complete first-pass transcription and, where appropriate, modern English translation exist, but one or more identified scholarly uncertainties remain.

## 3. Source-layer rules

The edition maintains three principal layers:

1. **Welsh manuscript transcription**: the base textual witness, preserving source forms and uncertainty.
2. **Modern English translation**: a translation of the Welsh itself, preserving ambiguity and contradiction.
3. **RDR historical English parallel**: a later historical translation, copy, paraphrase, expansion, or retelling. It is a separate witness, not the translation base.

Original-English manuscript entries on pages 73, 74, and 76 are labeled as English source transcription rather than translated Welsh. RDR wording is never inserted into uncertain Welsh merely because it is clearer. External evidence may identify a person, place, or context editorially, but it does not silently replace manuscript letterforms. Forms such as `Elisabeth`, `Chathreina`, `Cathreina`, `Cathrina`, `Margred`, `Hanah`, `Georg`, `Alizander`, and `Robert Davidson` remain source-specific.

## 8. Ffestiniog material

An independent research lead is the Ffestiniog branch membership register, DGS 106248099, images 394–407. Public indexing identifies David Roberts as entry 1 and records his baptism on May 24, 1846. Journal page 71 independently records baptism by Robert Evans on May 24, 1846 and states that the Ffestiniog Branch was entrusted to David’s care.

This is useful independent corroboration. An external handwriting comparison is no longer necessary for internal authorship, which is confirmed from signed samples within the journal. Any future external handwriting sample must nevertheless be independently attributable before it is used as evidence; branch leadership alone does not prove that David wrote the register.

## 9. 1852 hymn and poetry research lead

David Roberts is believed to be the `D. R.` associated with a hymn in the 1852 Welsh LDS hymnal. The journal independently establishes a strong poetic interest, acquaintance with `Gwilym Ystradau y Bardd`, and bardic instruction. It does not directly mention the 1852 hymn. The damaged page-69 verse remains unidentified, and no surviving text connects it securely to that hymn. The Roberts genealogy book may preserve hymn text, while the separate LDS Membership project contains a copy or searchable representation of the 1852 hymnal.

This remains a separate research track. The journal does not, by itself, prove the hymn attribution.

## 10. Robert Price and earlier ancestry

Page 72 identifies David Roberts’s father as Robert Roberts. It leaves David’s paternal grandfather unnamed. The manuscript block does not identify Robert Price or Rhys Roberts as that grandfather. Any later genealogy proposing such an identification must therefore remain a separate evidentiary layer rather than being inserted into the manuscript transcription.

## 11. What specialist review is and is not asking

The specialist is **not** being asked to:

- reconstruct erased text from guesswork;
- normalize historical names or spelling;
- reconcile every RDR conflict;
- verify ordinance mechanics;
- redo the full transcription; or
- answer every item individually.

The most useful review would:

- flag mistranslations that materially affect meaning;
- clarify difficult Welsh phrases and historical idiom;
- identify recognizable names or places;
- distinguish occupational, bardic, religious, ordinance, and kinship terminology;
- affirm where the existing uncertainty is appropriate; and
- identify anything that changes chronology, relationship, attribution, or interpretation.
