# Editor's Preface

David D. Roberts assembled this Book of Remembrance to preserve the history, faith, migrations, relationships, and records of his family. His work brought together family manuscripts, letters, church publications, recollections, photographs, and records that might otherwise have been separated or lost. This edition treats that achievement with gratitude and respect.

The original compiler worked with the evidence and translations available to him. Later access to the Welsh journal, the Robert D. Roberts English record, grave and cemetery evidence, family correspondence, and other documentary sources now permits some statements to be corrected, clarified, or enlarged. Those later findings are never silently substituted for the compiler's account. The historical compilation remains the base layer; modern editorial work appears separately and is labeled.

This first-pass draft establishes the complete editorial and section scaffold and completes the new edition's front matter. It also annotates the securely readable title, arrangement, source conventions, and selected front-matter claims. The family narrative beginning with Section A has not yet been rebuilt in this draft. That boundary is intentional: the surviving book contains 310 PDF pages, while the repository's established searchable extraction currently covers only PDF pages 1-68. Later sections require sequential source-image review before clean text can be responsibly published.

# How to Use This Edition

## Historical base text

Text labeled **Original compilation** represents what David D. Roberts recorded. Readable historical spelling and source-specific names are retained when they carry evidentiary value. Obvious mechanical inconsistencies may be regularized for reading only when the source is secure.

## Editorial categories

**Editor's correction** identifies a claim for which stronger evidence supports a different reading.

**Editor's clarification** distinguishes people, names, places, dates, or source layers that the original wording could leave ambiguous.

**Editor's note** supplies concise historical or documentary context without changing the compiler's claim.

**Evidence note** identifies the source basis for a correction or clarification.

## Uncertainty

Conflicting evidence is preserved. The terms *confirmed*, *probable*, *possible*, and *unresolved* describe the strength of an editorial conclusion; they do not erase the original statement. Missing or damaged source text is not reconstructed from a later retelling.

## Images

This draft contains placeholders only. Each placeholder identifies a subject, intended caption, and suggested provenance field. No replacement or synthetic image has been inserted.

# Original Compiler and Editorial Responsibility

The repository identifies the compiler as **David D. Roberts**, son of Robert D. Roberts and known in family context as "The Judge." The historical book itself commonly presents his name as **David R. Roberts**. Both forms are retained because the source form and canonical family identification answer different questions.

The compiler did not know Welsh and did not visit Wales. That context helps explain why he depended upon family translations, copied records, and information available through others. It is not a criticism of his care or purpose. Modern editorial corrections are benefits of later access, not judgments upon the original work.

## Original Title

### Original compilation

> Book of Remembrance of David and Catherine (uch Thomas ap Richard) Roberts, natives of Llanfrothen, Merionethshire, Wales, and their family.

### Editor's clarification

The title and arrangement establish a multi-branch family record rather than a biography of one person. This edition preserves the complete A-L plan even though only the front matter is rebuilt in this draft.

## Dedication

### Source condition note

The surviving dedication scan begins in the middle of a sentence. The missing opening is not supplied. The readable portion emphasizes the compiler's wish that descendants know the faith, pioneering work, and family example of their forebears and preserve their history as a united record.

### Original compilation - secure excerpts

> If a family is to live in the earth as a distinctive entity, it must write and preserve its history - the record of its individual members, and its branches - of their experiences and of the dealings of God with them.

> I feel an irresistible urge to perform this service of making available to posterity the records of our fathers and mothers.

### Editor's note

These statements explain the compiler's method and tone. The annotated edition retains that family-centered purpose while separating later evidence from his historical voice.

## Preface

### Original compilation - summary of the readable source

The compiler presents record keeping as a religious and family duty. He describes the earlier records kept by his grandfather David Roberts and his father Robert D. Roberts, explains his own effort to preserve and organize them, and asks descendants to continue the record. He also describes acquiring records, photographs, family-group information, and other materials over many years.

### Editor's note

The preface is an important provenance statement. It shows that the Book of Remembrance is itself a compilation from multiple earlier witnesses. Quotations copied into the book must therefore be compared with the surviving Welsh and English journals when exact wording, dates, or names matter.

## Explanatory Notes and Source Abbreviations

### Original compilation

The compiler explains that his grandfather David Roberts and father Robert D. Roberts both kept record books. He intended to preserve original material and marked copied passages with abbreviations. Securely readable abbreviations include:

- `D. Rec.` - record of David Roberts.
- `R. Rec.` - record of Robert D. Roberts.
- `R. MS.` - manuscripts of Robert D. Roberts.
- `R. L.` - copies of letters of Robert D. Roberts.
- `D. N.` - Deseret News.
- `J. H.` with a date - Journal History.
- `B. M.` with a date - British Mission record.
- `M. S.` with volume and page - Millennial Star.
- `C. S.` - church emigration or ship passenger register.
- `H. O.` - Historian's Office of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

### Editor's clarification

These abbreviations identify the cited source category, but do not always establish that a surviving quotation is verbatim. This edition will compare copied passages with an extant journal or publication where available.

## The Name "David R. Roberts"

### Original compilation

The compiler states that his grandfather was recorded as **David Roberts** in Wales, including the Ffestiniog branch record and Welsh Latter-day Saint hymnbook. He proposes that David added the middle initial `R` after reaching Utah, perhaps to distinguish himself from other men named David Roberts aboard the *S. Curling*.

### Editor's correction

The proposed timing is not fully established by the now-available signed evidence. The shared journal contains directly attributable signatures **David R Roberts** dated February 4 and April 6, 1858. They confirm his use of that form in Utah before his death on November 4, 1858, but do not establish exactly when or why he adopted it. The passenger explanation remains a family interpretation rather than a proven reason.

### Evidence note

- David Roberts shared journal, PDF page 73, signed entry dated February 4, 1858.
- David Roberts shared journal, PDF page 76, two signed records dated April 6, 1858.
- Book of Remembrance, source PDF page 3, compiler's explanatory note.

## The Hymn Initials "D. R."

### Original compilation

The compiler identifies `D. R.` in the Welsh Latter-day Saint hymnbook with David Roberts.

### Editor's clarification

The journal independently establishes David Roberts's strong poetic interest and bardic instruction. It does not directly identify the 1852 hymn, and the damaged verse on journal page 69 has not been connected to that hymn. The attribution remains a focused research question requiring comparison of the hymn text, printed attribution, genealogy-book text, and independent evidence.
