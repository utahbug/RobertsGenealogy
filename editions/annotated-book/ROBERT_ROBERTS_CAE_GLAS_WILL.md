## Will transcription

> In the Name of God Amen this 10th day of February 1823 I Robert Roberts of Cae Glas in the Parish of Clynnog in the County of Carnarvon farmer and [unclear] being weak in body but of perfect Mind and Memory thanks be given to God Therefore calling unto mind the Mortality of my body and knowing that it is appointed for all men once to die I make and ordain this my last will and Testament that is to say principally and first of all I give and recommend my Soul into the Hands of God that gave it, and my body I recommend to the earth to be buried in decent Christian Manner at the discretion of my Executors nothing doubting but at the General Resurrection I shall receive the same again by the mighty power of God. And as touching this worldly Estate wherewith it hath pleased God to bless me with in life I give, demise and dispose of the same in the following manner and Form.
>
> First I give and bequeath to my Daughter Margaret Roberts the wife of Humphrey Davies the sum of 2 Pounds; also I give to my Daughter Anne Roberts the wife of Robert Parry the sum of 2 Pounds; also I give to my Daughter Jane Roberts the wife of Samuel Griffiths the sum of 2 Pounds; also I give to my Son Robert Roberts the sum of 2 Pounds; also I give to my Son William Roberts the sum of 2 Pounds; also I give to my daughter Elizabeth Roberts the wife of Robert Hughes the sum of 2 Pounds. Solomon Roberts only 1 Shilling.
>
> Lastly I give and bequeath to my Daughter Eleanor Roberts all the rest and residue of my goods and Cattle Household [goods], whatsoever or wheresoever, or of what kind or nature and quality [soever], and without the same may be, [to] my Daughter Eleanor Roberts, and of these my last Will and Testament I named two corrections, and appoint my said Daughter Eleanor Roberts full and sole Executrix, Ratifying and confirming this and no other to be my last Will and Testament. For witness whereof I hereunto set my hand and seal in the tenth day of February 1823.

The phrase around the residue (`all the rest and residue ...`) is partly difficult in the supplied image, but the dispositive meaning and Eleanor Roberts's appointment as sole executrix are clear.

## Probate endorsement

On 25 June 1830 the will was proved in common form and administration was granted to the named executrix, written in the probate endorsement as **Ellin Roberts**. She was sworn concerning the goods, chattels, and credits of the deceased, stated not to amount in value to £200. The proving officer is the Reverend D. Williams, surrogate.

`Ellin Roberts` in the probate endorsement may be a record form or variant for the will's `Eleanor Roberts`; the images alone do not justify silently replacing either form.

## Comparison with the Book of Remembrance and David Roberts journal

### First nine-child list

The Book of Remembrance page 12 and David Roberts journal page 72 give the group:

`Salmon, Margred/Margaret, Robert, Catherine, Jane, Ann, Elinor, Elizabeth, William.`

The will names:

`Margaret, Anne, Jane, Robert, William, Elizabeth, Eleanor, Solomon.`

Seven names align directly or by ordinary spelling/form variation: Margaret, Anne/Ann, Jane, Robert, William, Elizabeth, and Eleanor/Elinor. **Solomon** is a strong candidate for the list's **Salmon**, although the will does not label Solomon's relationship. **Catherine** appears in the nine-child list but is not named in the will.

This close alignment is primary-source evidence that the first list describes the children or family group of **Robert Roberts of Cae Glas**, not a securely established Rhys Roberts. It does not identify Robert's father.

### Robert Roberts and Elizabeth Edwards eight-child list

The later list is:

`Robert (died at three months), Edward, Robert, Elinor, David, William, Daniel, John.`

Only Robert, Eleanor/Elinor, and William overlap with the will. The will does not resemble this list as a group and does not name David, Edward, Daniel, or John. It therefore does **not** support treating the will's beneficiaries as the children of Robert Roberts and Elizabeth Edwards.

### Evidentiary conclusion

The will strongly contradicts a confident assignment of the first child list to Rhys merely because the tentative sentence `I think grandfather's name was Rhys Roberts` precedes it in the later RDR account. It strongly supports Robert Roberts of Cae Glas as the parent represented by that first list. The identification is compelling but not absolute because the will does not name Robert's parents, wife, or every possible child, and the supplied images do not independently state that this testator was David Roberts's paternal grandfather.

## Cae Glas and ancestry boundary

The will is a primary, dated occurrence placing farmer Robert Roberts at **Cae Glas**, Clynnog, by 1823 and at his death before probate in 1830. The place-name is preserved without translating it as either `Green Field` or `Blue Field`.

The will proves the testator's residence and the relationships stated above. David Roberts's journal proves that David's father was Robert Roberts and gives the nine-name sibling group, but leaves David's paternal grandfather unnamed. The later genealogy book preserves a tentative Rhys tradition. None of these images identifies Robert Price or states the testator's parentage. Robert Price and the identity of Robert Roberts's father therefore remain unresolved.
